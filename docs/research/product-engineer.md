# What product-minded engineering teams look for

Research for [issue #352](https://github.com/3ixas/eliasb.dev/issues/352), informing “Where I'm headed”. This is evidence and framing guidance, not site copy or claims about Elias.

Method: Exa discovery across hiring, definitions, criticism and personal direction; Firecrawl retrieval of primary pages. Accessed **3 November 2026**. Seventeen cited sources from seventeen independent organisations/authors; seven job specifications across energy, maritime operations, education, healthcare, finance and consumer AI. “Recurring” means agreement in this sample, not measured industry prevalence. Publication dates and job status are recorded below.

## Short answer

- **Recurring:** The strongest common expectation is a continuous connection between choosing a useful problem, building a sound solution and checking what changed after release. Shipping is one stage. [S1], [S2], [S8], [S13], [S14]
- **Recurring:** Closeness to users means observable work: customer conversations, watching workflows, examining usage and support, scoping alternatives, and revisiting results. “Product-minded” by itself proves little. [S1], [S4], [S8], [S14]
- **Recurring, with disagreement:** Breadth usually sits alongside technical depth, reliability and domain understanding. There is a real risk of spreading expertise thinly; the sources do not agree that one person should replace every discipline. [S5], [S7], [S12], [S13], [S14], [S17]
- **Recurring:** Credibility comes from decisions and consequences: what someone built, why they chose it, what they left out, how it worked for users, and how they responded to evidence. A title or technology list is weaker evidence. [S3], [S4], [S8], [S10], [S14]
- **My inference:** Frame direction through the problems and responsibilities to grow into, with depth and range serving the same craft. Forward-looking work can mean making an emerging capability useful, dependable and understandable; novelty alone does not establish value. [S9], [S12], [S13], [S14]

## Findings by question

### 1. What teams want

**Recurring — PostHog, Opply, Companion.energy, Sequence and Toqua:** engineers contribute to what gets built, understand the intended benefit, make scope/technical trade-offs and remain responsible after launch. Orosz's older account describes the same combination working *with* product managers, designers and data specialists. [S1], [S2], [S4], [S8], [S13], [S14]

**Recurring — Linear, Janison, OpenAI and Toqua:** judgement includes collaboration and communication, rather than solitary heroics. Linear's phrase is “shaping the direction, making decisions, talking to users when needed, and communicating progress”. [S3], [S9], [S12], [S14]

**Single-source preferences:** Companion.energy explicitly includes much of product design in its engineering role; Janison expects assessment-specific AI evaluation; Neko's growth role prioritises conversion experiments. These are different roles, not a universal product-engineer checklist. [S8], [S9], [S11]

### 2. Meaning, standing and disagreement

**Recurring — Orosz, PostHog, Kavanagh and Toqua:** the common core is participating before a solution is fixed and following its effects after deployment. Definitions differ on compulsory full-stack work, design responsibility and whether a separate PM is present. OpenAI asks for product-minded engineers with a particular technical depth; Companion.energy combines frontend, product and design; Sequence explicitly retains product and design collaborators. [S1], [S2], [S5], [S8], [S12], [S13], [S14]

**Single-source, contested — Opply:** its argument treats the title as a way of changing expectations towards user outcomes. Kavanagh instead says “I recognise this as software engineering.” He finds the product label useful when shaping work and understanding results are explicit, supported responsibilities. These are competing interpretations, not proof that software engineers lack product judgement. [S4], [S5]

**Recurring criticism — Leckey and Unsworth:** Leckey reports an incomplete change at Corfix and warns against changing titles while leaving decision-making and measures unchanged. Unsworth, writing as a portfolio CPO, values customer-aware engineers but objects to turning one person into an entire operating model: “increasing somebody’s range” differs from “eliminating the need for expertise”. [S6], [S7]

**My inference:** the term is taken seriously by employers using it in live roles, and contested by practitioners over role inflation and superficial rebranding. This sample cannot establish how respected or overused it is across the profession. [S5], [S6], [S7], [S8], [S9], [S10], [S11], [S12], [S13], [S14]

### 3. What closeness to product, user and customer looks like

**Recurring — PostHog, Companion.energy and Toqua:** join customer calls; understand the task and constraints; identify a problem worth solving; choose a smaller useful scope; build; see whether it helped; return to the customer and iterate. PostHog adds instrumentation, session replay, support and closing the feedback loop with a change, a trackable issue or an explanation. [S1], [S8], [S14]

**Single-source — Neko:** “hypothesis, instrumentation, ship, read, keep or kill” describes its growth-experiment loop. This is more concrete than a general claim to care about impact. Kavanagh cautions that repeated dashboard visits can indicate confusion as well as usefulness: usage counts need interpretation. [S11], [S5]

**Single-source — Unsworth:** the buyer and the person using a B2B product can differ. **My inference:** “user” and “customer” should not be treated as interchangeable shorthand; direct customer contact is a stronger, separate claim from working with product data. [S7]

### 4. Depth and breadth

**Recurring — PostHog, OpenAI, Sequence and Toqua:** depth enables good product decisions. PostHog expects engineers to “Dive deep when they need to”; OpenAI pairs engineering depth with product judgement; Sequence describes distributed systems, reliability and optional deep database expertise; Toqua requires understanding ships and imperfect operational data. Breadth is following the problem across boundaries, not equal mastery of everything. [S1], [S12], [S13], [S14]

**Counterpoint, single-source — Hyperact's hosted panel synthesis:** it describes “trading some specialised depth for speed and independence” and warns of diluted expertise, while retaining a place for specialists. This is the host's unattributed synthesis, not an official position of the panellists' employers. Unsworth likewise argues for overlapping skills with different areas of genuine depth. [S17], [S7]

**My inference:** keep Depth a full strand. Connect understanding how systems behave to the quality of decisions and the dependability of the resulting product; do not imply generalism removes the need for expertise.

### 5. Believable signals and hollow claims

**Recurring — Linear, Opply, Companion.energy, Coddy and Toqua:** show work and explain reasoning. Linear uses paid real-project trials to observe decisions, collaboration and response to feedback. Opply asks whether candidates interrogate assumptions, stop an unsuitable feature and describe what changed. Companion.energy asks for a live link, screenshots, video or repository plus the decisions and reasons; its case tests problem choice, design and implementation. Coddy values products people used or paid for. Toqua uses an end-to-end technical/product exercise. [S3], [S4], [S8], [S10], [S14]

**Single-source — Neko:** it asks for features that moved a number and the ability to distinguish an experiment result from noise. **My inference:** a shipped artefact supports delivery, but does not by itself demonstrate customer understanding, good prioritisation or measurable benefit. [S11]

**Recurring caution — Kavanagh and Leckey:** a title without supported authority, follow-up time or changed behaviour is hollow. **My inference:** trait labels and claims of “impact” need the decisions and evidence beneath them; the research does not establish a blacklist of universally disliked words. [S5], [S6]

### 6. Describing direction without an application

**Observed examples, not hiring evidence:** Josh Comeau's personal site says “These days, I'm a solo indie hacker focused on empowering up-and-coming software developers.” It connects a current focus to concrete teaching and products. Maggie Appleton's March 2025 now entry says “I’ve learned I like this shape of work”, then specifies complex scientific workflows, information-dense interfaces and making model reasoning visible. [S16], [S15]

**Limit:** Appleton separately signals job availability later in that entry; the whole page is not a model of non-availability. The useful example is the passage identifying a preferred *shape of work*. Comeau describes an established present focus, not a two-to-three-year aspiration. **My inference:** use that specificity and connection to actual work, while keeping Elias's section explicitly directional; neither example proves how hiring managers will read it.

### 7. Forward-looking without hype

**Recurring — Janison, OpenAI, Sequence and Toqua:** connect a capability to a concrete human task and its constraints. Janison couples new model experimentation with assessment validity and privacy; OpenAI couples emerging AI with useful consumer experiences, reliability and trust; Sequence describes auditable approval workflows and resilient billing; Toqua applies ship models to operational decisions and emissions. [S9], [S12], [S13], [S14]

**My inference:** the useful vocabulary concerns exploring, prototyping, testing, making understandable, improving and operating reliably. Emerging tools are a means. “Future of”, “cutting-edge” and “AI-native” appear in hiring language, but their presence is not evidence of meaningful forward direction; name the practical possibility and quality bar instead.

## What this means for the section

These are **my inferences**, not finished copy.

- **Line:** connect product decisions, understanding users and dependable delivery as one continuing practice. Useful concepts: useful problems, product judgement, technical understanding, working software, learning from use. Keep the direction larger than a job title; no company names, availability signal or comparison with other organisations.
- **Judgement:** emphasise choosing and shaping worthwhile work using evidence, constraints and trade-offs. Raw vocabulary: understand the problem; decide what matters; scope; learn; prioritise; what changed.
- **Depth:** emphasise understanding systems well enough to make sound choices and build things people can rely on. Raw vocabulary: fundamentals; why it behaves that way; reliability; consequences; domain understanding.
- **Range:** emphasise carrying a problem through connected stages and crossing technical boundaries when the work needs it. Raw vocabulary: shape, build, ship, operate, follow through, improve. Avoid implying expert-level design, research, commercial and engineering competence all at once.
- **Forward direction:** express interest in what better tools and deeper understanding make possible for people. The sources support experimental work with a practical quality bar; they do not justify adding a domain, technology or customer claim absent from Elias's account.

## Tension with the proofs

The supplied proofs stand unchanged. These are limits on what their wording can establish, not judgements about unrecorded experience.

- **Judgement:** split tests and dashboards guiding engineering work support evidence informing priorities. They do not establish direct user/customer conversations, independent problem discovery, commercial ownership or measured improvement.
- **Depth:** progression from zero C# to production within a substantial pricing system supports learning and technical exposure. Duration and project count alone do not establish deep architectural expertise, correctness responsibility or the reasoning behind consequential decisions.
- **Range:** 57 user stories to a live site supports movement from requirements to deployment. It does not establish validated demand, paying users, customer contact, post-launch learning or long-term operation.
- **Decision for Elias:** whether customer closeness and post-release learning are directions to grow towards. This evidence cannot turn them into achievements already demonstrated by the proofs. The same distinction applies to depth: preserve a substantial ambition without converting fast learning into a claim of mastery.

## Limits

This is a purposive sample of public hiring and practitioner writing, biased towards autonomous software teams and positive employer self-description. Jobs describe expectations, not observed working conditions or validated predictors of hiring success. The seven specifications cover small teams and established employers (Janison describes a 25-year history; OpenAI describes products used by millions), but do not establish enterprise-wide norms. Developer-tool companies supply two sources, PostHog and Linear; personal examples add a developer/design audience bias. [S3], [S9], [S12], [S15], [S16]

The common behaviour pattern is better supported than predictions about AI or a universal future team structure. Critical sources establish disagreement, not its prevalence. No source directly tests this homepage, its audience reaction or the proposed three-strand structure. Two discovered jobs were excluded after live 404s (Parafin and Bigblue); the PostHog hub yielded no useful body, so its handbook was retrieved instead. All cited material was opened; quotations were checked against page text. Undated pages remain undated rather than borrowing search-index dates.
