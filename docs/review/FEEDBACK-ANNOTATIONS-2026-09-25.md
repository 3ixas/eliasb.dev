# Personal site interaction and visual review — 25 September 2026

## Source

Ten browser annotations on the live site at `https://www.eliasb.dev/#outside-work`, supplied on 25 September 2026. The screenshots are page evidence; the user’s comments below define the requested changes.

## Annotations

1. **Site-wide writing voice.** Remove robotic or overly formal language across the whole public site. In particular, remove the GitHub sentence “Private contributions are part of the total. I keep their repositories private” and the fantasy-football sentence about other managers’ names. Make remaining wording warmer and more personal; remove lines that add no useful meaning.
2. **Homepage opening.** Follow the two-part headline rhythm Elias likes in Commissioner Design: show the background, type a meaningful first clause through a comma, let the rest of the sentence land, bring in the highlight, then reveal the rest of the page. Slow the current jittery entrance, retain the original meaning in a more suitable sentence, and remove “Replay the opening”; a page reload should play the opening again.
3. **Theme control.** Start from the device’s light/dark preference and follow live device-setting changes while the site is open. Replace the half-circle glyph with one custom, animated sun-to-moon control.
4. **Brand mark.** Use an orange slash between the white/light-theme-appropriate E and B letters wherever the site displays its E/B logo.
5. **GitHub calendar.** Make the full year larger and easier to read, with clear months and year context. A swipeable full-year view is acceptable on small screens. Add subtle detail/reaction when a visitor explores it.
6. **Site-wide motion.** Make the whole site feel responsive as visitors scroll and move over its content. Use purposeful motion rather than corny or overwhelming effects.
7. **Imagery.** Audit every major section for a meaningful visual opportunity, including the opening/header area and contact. Use imagery to make people, places, projects, and interests easier to understand.
8. **Weekly history.** Prefer surprising, little-known, quirky facts over familiar news-headline-style entries. Where a relevant image can be sourced, show and cache it with the weekly selection.
9. **Career story.** Make About tell the story behind the workplaces and transitions instead of repeating a résumé. Give the BNP Paribas role a more informative software-engineering description without reducing it to a C# specialty.
10. **Contact.** Use the contact section as an example of where purposeful imagery and motion can make a text-heavy section more engaging.

## Scope note

Earlier tickets already improved several of these areas. The new spec records remaining gaps and additions without discarding those delivered changes or inventing facts.
